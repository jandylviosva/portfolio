What a Shopify VA Can Do for Your Store

By Jandyl Vios · Shopify Virtual Assistant · August 2026

← Back to Portfolio

If you're running a Shopify store, you already know the feeling: your to-do list never ends. You're uploading products, responding to customers, processing orders, tweaking your theme, researching competitors — all while trying to actually grow your business.

A Shopify Virtual Assistant (VA) takes the backend operations off your plate so you can focus on the work that actually moves the needle — marketing, partnerships, and scaling.

In this post, I'll walk you through exactly what a Shopify VA does, which tasks to delegate first, and what to look for when hiring one.

What Is a Shopify Virtual Assistant?

A Shopify Virtual Assistant is a remote professional who specializes in managing and operating Shopify stores. Unlike a general VA who handles emails and scheduling, a dedicated Shopify VA understands the Shopify platform deeply — the admin panel, theme editor, Liquid code, app ecosystem, and the workflows that keep an e-commerce store running smoothly.

Think of a Shopify VA as your behind-the-scenes store manager. They know where everything is, how everything connects, and how to keep things moving — without you having to explain it from scratch every time.

A good Shopify VA doesn't just complete tasks you assign — they can run store operations independently, flag issues before they become problems, and suggest improvements based on what they see in your data.

The Tasks a Shopify VA Can Handle

Here's a breakdown of the core areas a Shopify VA covers:

📦 Product Listing & PDP Building

Writing product titles, descriptions, adding variants, tags, and pricing. Building optimized product detail pages (PDPs) that convert.

🛒 Order Processing & Fulfillment

Managing orders end-to-end — processing, tracking, coordinating with suppliers, handling cancellations and refunds.

💬 Customer Support

Responding to customer emails and chats professionally and promptly, using tools like Gorgias or email.

🎨 Theme Customization

Editing your Shopify theme using the Theme Editor and Liquid code to match your brand without hiring a full developer.

📊 Inventory Management

Keeping stock levels accurate, setting up low-stock alerts, and coordinating restocks with your suppliers.

🌍 Store Localization

Translating and adapting your store content for international markets — product descriptions, checkout copy, and more.

Which Tasks Should You Delegate First?

If you're new to working with a VA, start with high-volume, repeatable tasks that eat up your time but don't require your unique judgment. The best first tasks to hand off are:

  1. Product uploads — especially if you're adding new products regularly. This alone can save 5–10 hours per week.
  2. Order management — processing, tracking updates, and basic fulfillment coordination.
  3. Customer email responses — using templates and SOPs, a VA can handle 80% of support tickets without escalating to you.

Once your VA is settled and you've built trust, you can expand to more complex tasks like theme edits, app integrations, and store optimization.

What to Look For When Hiring a Shopify VA

Not all Shopify VAs are created equal. When evaluating candidates, look for:

  • A portfolio showing real Shopify stores they've worked on
  • Specific tool familiarity — Shopify Admin, theme editor, apps like Klaviyo, Loox, Recharge, Printful
  • Experience with SOPs and structured task management (Trello, ClickUp, Google Sheets)
  • Video or written testimonials from past clients
  • Clear communication and response time during the hiring process
Pro tip: Before hiring, ask candidates to walk you through how they would set up a product page from a spec sheet. The answer tells you more than any resume.

How Much Does a Shopify VA Cost?

Rates vary depending on experience, scope, and arrangement. Here's a general range:

  • Hourly: $5–$15/hr for experienced Shopify VAs from the Philippines
  • Project-based: For example, a full store setup can start at $400
  • Monthly retainer: For ongoing store management, many VAs offer retainer packages that are more cost-effective than hourly billing

Compare this to hiring a local e-commerce manager at $3,000–$5,000/month, and it's easy to see why so many store owners turn to remote VAs.
