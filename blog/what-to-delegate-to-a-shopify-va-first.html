What to Delegate to a Shopify VA First

By Jandyl Vios · Shopify Virtual Assistant · January 2027

← Back to Portfolio

Hiring help is the easy part. Knowing what to hand over is harder, and many owners either give too little ("just answer emails") or too much ("take care of the store") on day one. Both lead to disappointment.

The best approach is to start with tasks that are repetitive, clearly defined and low risk. Here's a practical order.

Start Here: Easy Wins

📦 Product uploads

Titles, descriptions, images, variants, prices and tags, following your template. It's repetitive, easy to check and easy to fix.

🧾 Order processing

Reviewing orders, flagging problems, updating tracking and coordinating with your supplier or fulfillment partner.

💬 Customer support

Answering common questions by email or chat using your approved replies, and escalating anything unusual to you.

🗂️ Inventory updates

Keeping stock, availability and out-of-stock messages accurate.

Next: Store Improvements

  • Product page upgrades: better descriptions, image order, size guides, shipping and returns information.
  • Policy and trust pages: refund, shipping, privacy and contact pages that are easy to find.
  • App setup: reviews, email and SMS sign-up forms, subscriptions and sizing tools.
  • Small theme edits: section changes, banners, menus and layout fixes.

Later: Higher-Stakes Work

  • Theme and Liquid changes that affect the whole store.
  • Marketing setup: Google Merchant Center, product feeds, tracking and ad accounts.
  • Localization: translating and adapting the store for new markets.

These need more trust and more care. Hand them over once the VA has shown they follow your standards on the simpler work.

Keep these with you at the start: payment and bank settings, pricing strategy, refunds above an amount you set, and anything that emails your customers. Add them back only when you're comfortable.

How to Hand Over a Task Well

  1. Show it once. A short screen recording of you doing the task beats a long explanation.
  2. Write a few rules. Tone of voice, naming format, image standards and what to do when unsure.
  3. Give an example. One finished product page or reply that shows "this is what good looks like".
  4. Set permissions carefully. Use a staff account with only the access the task needs, not your owner login.
  5. Review early, then step back. Check the first batch closely, then spot-check as trust grows.

Your First-Week Plan

  • Day 1: access, tools and communication channel set up.
  • Days 2 to 3: a small batch of product uploads or support replies for review.
  • Days 4 to 5: feedback, then a larger batch.
  • Week 2: add the next task from the "store improvements" list.

The goal isn't to hand off everything. It's to buy back the hours you spend on work that doesn't need you.
